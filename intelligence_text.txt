 Combined Intelligence and DataLake Architecture Manual PlantSafety Platform · Version 1.0 Title page Document Title: Combined Intelligence Architecture Manual Program: PlantSafety Platform Division: Enterprise Architecture · Intelligence &amp; Analytics Version: 1.0 — June 2026 Authors: Enterprise Architecture Intelligence &amp; Analytics Engineering Data Engineering Platform Engineering Classification: Internal — Architecture &amp; Engineering Preface The Intelligence subsystem is the analytical and predictive brain of the PlantSafety platform. It consumes events and data from operational modules, transforms them into features and signals, trains and executes predictive models, and feeds insights back into the platform. This manual describes the architecture and design of the combined Intelligence system, with a specific focus on how it interacts with: The DataLake (analytical storage backbone) Azure (cloud execution and integration environment) PlantSafety modules (Diagnostics, Auditing, Routing, Scheduling, Predictive, etc.) This is a design and architecture manual , not a rules or governance document. It explains what exists , how it is structured , and how the parts work together . Rules, policies, and enforcement mechanisms are intentionally out of scope and belong in separate Rules Manuals. The intended audience includes architects, Intelligence engineers, data engineers, module owners, and platform engineers who design, build, or integrate with the Intelligence subsystem. Executive summary The PlantSafety Intelligence subsystem is a modular, layered architecture that sits between operational modules and analytical storage, using the DataLake as its central substrate and Azure as its execution environment. At a high level: PlantSafety modules (Diagnostics, Auditing, Routing, Scheduling, etc.) emit events and data. Intelligence ingestion pipelines capture these signals and write them into the DataLake (Raw → Cleansed → Curated → ML zones). Intelligence processing pipelines transform, enrich, and aggregate data into analytics-ready and ML-ready datasets. Predictive models are trained and executed using data from the DataLake , with artifacts and features stored in the ML zone. Insights and predictions are published back to PlantSafety modules and exposed through analytics and dashboards. Azure services (e.g., ADLS Gen2, EventHub, compute engines, ML services) provide the runtime and integration fabric, encapsulated in an Azure-specific implementation layer. The architecture is deliberately decoupled : Intelligence domain logic is independent of any specific cloud provider. DataLake is a conceptual and structural design, with Azure bindings implemented in a separate project. Operational modules interact with Intelligence through well-defined contracts and integration points. This manual describes: The Intelligence subsystem structure The Intelligence– DataLake interaction model The Intelligence–Azure integration layer The Intelligence–PlantSafety module integration patterns The processing, ML, and analytics flows that tie everything together Part I — Intelligence subsystem overview (expanded) 1. Intelligence subsystem purpose (in depth) The Intelligence subsystem is the analytical and predictive brain of PlantSafety. It sits beside the operational modules, not inside them, and its purpose is to continuously turn raw operational activity into foresight. 1.1 Transform operational data into insights, predictions, and recommendations What this really means : Inputs: Diagnostics runs, health checks, sensor readings Audit events, approvals, configuration changes Routing decisions, workflow transitions, timing data Scheduling plans, execution logs, delays, resource usage Transformations: Correlate events across modules and time Compute derived metrics (e.g., failure rates, lead times, compliance ratios) Build features that capture behavior, patterns, and risk signals Outputs: Risk scores (per asset, per line, per site, per shift) Early warning indicators (e.g., “this line is trending toward unsafe operation”) Recommended actions (e.g., reschedule, reroute, inspect, slow down, escalate) Intelligence is the layer that answers questions like: “What is likely to fail next?” “Where is safety degrading before an incident occurs?” “Which routes or schedules are systematically unsafe?” It does this by reading from the DataLake , not from live operational databases. 1.2 Enable cross-module analytics Operational modules are bounded contexts ; Intelligence is deliberately cross-cutting . Diagnostics knows about tests and health. Routing knows about workflows and approvals. Scheduling knows about time and resources. Auditing knows about who did what, when, and under which configuration. Intelligence: Joins these perspectives into unified analytical views . Builds datasets like “ AssetSafetyHistory ”, “ RouteRiskProfile ”, “ ScheduleStressIndex ”. Uses these cross-module views to detect patterns that no single module can see alone . This is why Intelligence is tightly coupled to the DataLake : the lake is where cross-module data is co-located, normalized, and made analyzable. 1.3 Provide a predictive safety layer The predictive safety layer is where Intelligence moves from describing the past to anticipating the future. Examples of what lives here: Early warning models Predict increased risk for a line, asset, or shift based on recent diagnostics, routing decisions, and schedule pressure. Risk scoring models Assign continuous or categorical risk scores to entities (assets, routes, schedules, operators, shifts). Anomaly detection Identify unusual patterns in diagnostics, routing, or scheduling that historically correlate with incidents or near-misses. Architecturally: These models are trained on Curated + ML zone data. Their artifacts and features are stored in the ML zone . Their predictions are written back into Curated and/or module-facing stores. Intelligence is the predictive skin over the entire PlantSafety platform. 1.4 Support long-horizon trend analysis and historical investigations Operational systems are optimized for current state and recent history . Intelligence is optimized for deep history and longitudinal analysis . Uses the DataLake to store years of events and metrics at low cost. Enables questions like: “How has safety performance evolved over the last 3 years by plant, line, and shift?” “What patterns consistently precede high-risk periods?” “Which combinations of routing and scheduling decisions correlate with incidents?” Architecturally: Long-horizon datasets live in the Curated zone . Trend and history views are built as derived datasets and aggregations . Intelligence exposes these to reporting, dashboards, and further modeling. 1.5 Serve as consumer and producer of analytical DataLake data Intelligence is both: A consumer of DataLake data (for analytics and ML), and A producer of new analytical artifacts (features, predictions, aggregates). As a consumer: Reads Raw → Cleansed → Curated → ML datasets. Uses them for feature engineering, model training, and analysis. As a producer: Writes: Enriched Curated datasets (e.g., “ SafetyRiskByRouteAndShift ”). Feature sets and training splits into the ML zone. Predictions and scores back into Curated or module-facing outputs. This makes Intelligence a first-class participant in the DataLake lifecycle, not just a passive reader. 1.6 Not an operational system of record Intelligence: Does not own the canonical truth for operational entities (assets, routes, schedules, audits). Does not participate in transactional workflows or enforce operational invariants. Does not replace Diagnostics, Auditing, Routing, or Scheduling. Instead, it: Observes what those modules do. Learns from their history. Feeds them better information so they can make better decisions. This separation is critical: Intelligence is analytical and predictive , not transactional. 2. High-level Intelligence architecture (in depth) Now let’s go layer by layer and treat each as a component with clear responsibilities, inputs, and outputs . 2.1 Ingestion Layer Role: Bridge between operational/event world and the DataLake Raw zone . Responsibilities: Receive data from: Module event streams (e.g., Diagnostics events, Routing events). Batch exports (e.g., nightly snapshots of key tables). External feeds (e.g., regulatory data, reference catalogs). Normalize transport : Convert incoming payloads into stable, documented formats (JSON, CSV, Parquet). Attach ingestion metadata (source, timestamp, correlation ID). Write to DataLake Raw zone : Persist payloads in a source- and domain-organized folder structure . Preserve original content for replay and reprocessing. Inputs: Events, files, API payloads from modules and external systems. Outputs: Files in /raw/... in the DataLake . Metadata entries describing each dataset and its origin. The Ingestion Layer is append-only from Intelligence’s perspective. 2.2 Processing Layer Role: Turn Raw into Cleansed and Curated —the “data refinery” of Intelligence. Responsibilities: Raw → Cleansed: Validate structure and types. Normalize units, encodings, and formats. Deduplicate records. Add technical fields (ingestion time, processing time, pipeline ID). Cleansed → Curated: Join across modules (e.g., Diagnostics + Routing + Scheduling). Compute derived metrics and indicators. Aggregate at meaningful grains (asset, route, shift, day, plant). Align data to analytical models (e.g., “ SafetyRiskContext ”, “ MaintenanceHistory ”). Inputs: Raw zone datasets. Existing Cleansed datasets. Reference data (e.g., asset hierarchies, plant structures). Outputs: Cleansed datasets in /cleansed/... . Curated datasets in /curated/... . The Processing Layer is where cross-module semantics are encoded into data. 2.3 ML &amp; Analytics Layer Role: Convert Curated data into features, models, and analytical outputs . Responsibilities: Feature engineering: Build feature sets from Curated datasets. Apply temporal logic (lags, rolling windows, seasonality). Encode categorical variables, handle missing values. Dataset preparation: Split feature sets into training, validation, and test datasets. Persist them in the ML zone for reproducibility. Model training and execution: Train models using ML frameworks. Evaluate and select models. Execute models for batch or near-real-time scoring. Artifact management: Store model binaries, metadata, and evaluation metrics in the ML zone. Inputs: Curated datasets. ML zone feature sets (for retraining, experiments). Outputs: Feature sets and splits in /ml/features/... . Model artifacts in /ml/models/... . Analytical datasets (e.g., “ RiskScoreHistory ”) in Curated. This layer is where Intelligence becomes predictive , not just descriptive. 2.4 Insight &amp; Output Layer Role: Expose Intelligence results back to PlantSafety and to human consumers. Responsibilities: Publish predictions and scores: Risk scores per asset, route, schedule, shift, etc. Anomaly flags and early warnings. Publish metrics and aggregates: Safety KPIs, performance indicators, trend metrics. Cross-module summaries (e.g., “Safety vs Throughput”). Expose outputs: Write back into Curated datasets. Provide module-facing outputs (e.g., via APIs, queues, or shared stores). Feed dashboards and reporting tools. Inputs: Model outputs from the ML &amp; Analytics Layer. Aggregated datasets from the Processing Layer. Outputs: Prediction datasets in Curated. Module-facing signals (e.g., “ HighRiskRoute ”, “ ScheduleUnderStress ”). Dashboard-ready views. This layer is the interface between Intelligence and the rest of the platform. 2.5 Integration Layer (Azure) Role: Provide the cloud execution and integration fabric while keeping Intelligence domain logic cloud-agnostic. Responsibilities: Storage bindings: ADLS Gen2 clients for reading/writing DataLake zones. Event and stream bindings: EventHub, Service Bus, or equivalent for ingestion. Compute bindings: Batch and streaming compute (Spark, Databricks, Synapse, containers). ML bindings: Azure ML or equivalent for training and deployment. Security and secrets: KeyVault , managed identities, access tokens. Observability: Logging, metrics, traces, health checks. Inputs/Outputs: Wraps all I/O between Intelligence and Azure services. Presents clean abstractions to the Intelligence layers above. This layer ensures that if the cloud provider changes, Intelligence architecture remains intact and only bindings are swapped. 2.6 Metadata &amp; Catalog Layer Role: Provide structural and semantic context for all Intelligence-related data and artifacts. Responsibilities: Dataset metadata: Names, schemas, locations, partitions, retention characteristics. Lineage: Which pipeline produced which dataset from which inputs. Model metadata: Which feature sets and datasets were used to train which models. Model versions, evaluation metrics, deployment status (conceptually). Discoverability: Allow engineers and analysts to find the right datasets and models. Traceability: Enable tracing from a prediction back to the data and model that produced it. Inputs: Events from ingestion, processing, ML, and output pipelines. Outputs: Catalog entries and lineage graphs that describe the Intelligence data universe. This layer is what makes the Intelligence subsystem understandable and navigable over time. Conceptual Relationship (Deep Architecture View) The Intelligence subsystem and the DataLake form a dual system : DataLake = analytical storage backbone Holds all analytical data across zones Stores raw, historical, enriched, and ML-ready datasets Provides the substrate for analytics, ML, and cross-module insights Intelligence = analytical brain Ingests data into the DataLake Transforms data across zones Builds features, trains models, computes predictions Publishes insights back to modules 3.1 Why Intelligence writes to the DataLake Intelligence is responsible for: Capturing operational events Normalizing and cleansing them Enriching and aggregating them Producing analytical and ML-ready datasets These outputs must be stored in a central, scalable, low-cost, historical repository — the DataLake . 3.2 Why Intelligence reads from the DataLake Intelligence requires: Long-horizon historical data Cross-module datasets High-volume event streams ML feature sets and training splits Curated analytical datasets These are all produced inside the DataLake , so Intelligence reads from it as its single analytical source of truth . 3.3 Why Intelligence controls zone transitions The DataLake itself is passive — it stores files. Intelligence is active — it performs all transformations. Only Intelligence knows: How Diagnostics relates to Routing How Scheduling interacts with Auditing How predictive models consume features How cross-module semantics should be joined Therefore, Intelligence owns: Raw → Cleansed Cleansed → Curated Curated → ML The DataLake Design Manual describes the storage architecture. This manual describes how Intelligence uses that architecture. 4. Each execution pattern corresponds to a different class of analytical or ML activity. 8.1 Batch Processing (Deep Detail) Purpose Perform large-scale, periodic transformations and computations. Typical Cadences Nightly Hourly Every 15 minutes (for semi-real-time analytics) Responsibilities Transform Raw → Cleansed → Curated Recompute aggregates (daily, weekly, monthly) Build feature sets for ML Generate training datasets Refresh analytical tables Architectural Characteristics Runs on Spark, Databricks, Synapse, or containerized compute Reads/writes large volumes of DataLake files Optimized for throughput, not latency Why batch matters Batch processing is the backbone of: Historical analytics Trend analysis ML training data preparation Cross-module aggregations 8.2 Streaming / Near-Real-Time Processing (Deep Detail) Purpose Process events as they arrive to maintain fresh analytical state . Batch Builds the historical backbone. Streaming Maintains real-time analytical state. Interactive Explores, experiments, and prototypes. Together They form a complete analytical ecosystem : Code [Streaming] → Real-time features → Real-time scoring [Batch] → Historical features → Batch scoring + training [Interactive] → Exploration → New features → New models Azure provides the runtime , but Intelligence provides the logic . Glossary This glossary defines all major architectural terms used throughout the manual. A ADLS Gen2 — Azure Data Lake Storage Gen2; the physical implementation of the DataLake storage layer. Analytics Layer — Intelligence layer responsible for aggregations, metrics, and analytical views. Append-Only — A write pattern where data is only added, never modified or deleted. B Batch Processing — Periodic, large-scale data processing used for transformations and ML training. Batch Export — A structured snapshot of operational data delivered on a schedule. C Cleansed Zone — DataLake zone containing validated, normalized, and type-correct data. Curated Zone — DataLake zone containing analytics-ready, enriched datasets. Compute Topology — The arrangement of compute engines used by Intelligence. D Data Contract — A structural and semantic agreement defining the shape of data exchanged between systems. DataLake — The analytical storage backbone used by Intelligence. Diagnostics Module — Module responsible for tests, health checks, and diagnostic runs. E Event Stream — A continuous flow of domain events emitted by operational modules. Event Time — The timestamp when an event actually occurred . F Feature Engineering — The process of transforming Curated data into ML-ready features. Feature Set — A structured dataset containing engineered features for ML training. G GPU/CPU Pools — Compute resources used for ML training. I Ingestion Layer — Intelligence layer responsible for capturing and writing data to the Raw zone. Intelligence Subsystem — The analytical and predictive layer of PlantSafety. L Lineage — Metadata describing the origin and transformation history of a dataset. ML Zone — DataLake zone containing feature sets, training splits, and model artifacts. M Model Artifact — A trained model binary and its associated metadata. Model Serving — The execution of trained models to generate predictions. O Operational Module — A PlantSafety subsystem responsible for transactional workflows. Output Pipeline — A pipeline that publishes predictions, metrics, or alerts. P Partitioning — Organizing data into subdirectories for performance and scalability. Predictive Model — A machine learning model used to generate predictions or risk scores. R Raw Zone — Immutable DataLake zone storing data exactly as received. Real-Time Scoring — Applying models to streaming data for immediate predictions. S Sandbox — An isolated environment for experimentation and prototyping. Scoring Pipeline — A pipeline that applies a trained model to new data. Streaming Processing — Continuous processing of event streams. T Temporal Alignment — The process of synchronizing events across modules by time. Training Dataset — A dataset used to train ML models. W Windowing — A technique for grouping events into time-based windows for analysis.
